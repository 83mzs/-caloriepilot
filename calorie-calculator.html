Estimated Daily Maintenance Calories

What Is a Calorie Calculator?

A calorie calculator estimates how much energy you may need each day using factors such as age, body weight, height, sex and physical activity. The result can be used as a starting point for understanding maintenance calories.

How Does the Calorie Calculator Work?

CaloriePilot estimates Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and then applies an activity factor to estimate Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE).

What Is BMR?

BMR, or Basal Metabolic Rate, is an estimate of the energy your body uses at rest to support essential functions such as breathing and circulation.

What Is TDEE?

TDEE, or Total Daily Energy Expenditure, estimates total daily energy use including activity and movement.

What Are Maintenance Calories?

Maintenance calories are an estimate of the daily energy intake that would maintain body weight under typical conditions. Actual needs can change as body weight, activity and lifestyle change.

Are Calorie Calculator Results Exact?

No. These are estimates, not measurements. Real energy requirements vary between individuals, so use the result as a starting point rather than an exact prescription.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories should I eat a day?

There is no single number for everyone. Your estimated calorie needs depend on age, weight, height, sex and activity. Use the calculator above for an individualized estimate.

How many calories do I need to maintain my weight?

Your estimated TDEE can be used as a starting estimate for maintenance calories. Monitor changes over time because actual needs can differ.

Can a calorie calculator help with weight loss?

It can estimate maintenance calories, which can help you understand the relationship between food intake and energy needs. A sustainable plan should account for individual circumstances.

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR estimates energy used at rest. TDEE adds activity and daily movement to estimate total energy expenditure.

Should I recalculate my calories after losing weight?

It can be useful to review your estimate when body weight or activity changes significantly because calorie needs may change too.
