What Is BMR?

BMR stands for Basal Metabolic Rate. It is an estimate of the amount of energy your body uses while at rest to support essential functions such as breathing, circulation and maintaining body temperature.

How Is BMR Calculated?

CaloriePilot uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation to estimate BMR. The calculation uses information such as age, body weight, height and sex.

Why Is BMR Important?

Knowing your estimated BMR can help you understand your basic energy requirements. It can also be used as a starting point when estimating total daily energy expenditure.

BMR vs TDEE

BMR estimates energy used by your body at rest. TDEE, or Total Daily Energy Expenditure, takes physical activity and daily movement into account.

Are BMR Results Exact?

No. BMR calculations provide estimates rather than exact measurements. Individual energy requirements can vary depending on body composition, activity, lifestyle and other factors.

CaloriePilot provides general informational estimates and does not provide medical advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does BMR mean?

BMR means Basal Metabolic Rate. It estimates the energy your body uses at rest to maintain essential functions.

Does BMR change with age?

BMR can change over time as factors such as body weight, body composition and age change.

Is BMR the same as maintenance calories?

No. BMR represents energy used at rest, while maintenance calories generally include energy used through daily activity and movement.

Can BMR be used to calculate TDEE?

Yes. A BMR estimate can be combined with an activity factor to estimate Total Daily Energy Expenditure, commonly called TDEE.
